# Australian regulatory and platform requirements for live trading (research)

Researched 2026-11-13 from regulator, legislation and Apple pages. **This is engineering research, not legal advice** (see section 9). Tags like [R4] point to **Sources**. `UNCONFIRMED` means not verified from an official source in this pass. Section numbers of the Corporations Act [R1] are cited as quoted in ASIC's guides unless a legislation source is tagged.

## 0. Map: which feature triggers what

| Feature | Likely legal characterisation | What it requires | Key source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper trading, simulated portfolios | No financial product is acquired, so no dealing. Displayed results and any trade ideas are still representations (and possibly advice) | No misleading conduct; non-actual performance labelled; advice rules if ideas are given | [R15] |
| Manual live order sent to the user's own broker/exchange | **Arranging**, which is a form of **dealing** (a financial service), where the asset is a financial product | AFS licence with a dealing authorisation, or acting as (authorised) representative of a licensee, or an exemption | [R2] |
| AI research, signals, trade proposals | **Financial product advice**: general, or personal if the user's circumstances are (or appear) considered | AFS licence/representative; general advice warning; personal advice duties | [R2][R3] |
| Agent trades automatically within user limits | Dealing as agent plus trading discretion: **managed discretionary account (MDA)** for retail clients | AFS licence with MDA authorisations, MDA contract, investment program, personal advice, reviews | [R4][R5] |
| Any of the above for crypto | Depends token by token whether it is a financial product today; platform regime from April 2027; AML/CTF registration questions now | See section 4 | [R6][R10][R11] |

## 1. Routing users' orders to a third-party broker or exchange

- **Dealing includes arranging.** "'Arranging' for a person to engage in certain conduct, such as applying for or acquiring a financial product ..., constitutes dealing unless: (a) the actions concerned amount to providing financial product advice (s766C(2)); or (b) the conduct is exempt from the definition of dealing, for example, if the clerks and cashiers exemption applies (s766A(3))" [R2, RG 36.9].
- **ASIC names order routing specifically:** "the activities of an order router will generally involve arranging (i.e. where a service provider takes steps to bring into effect an acquisition or disposal of a financial product, albeit that another intermediary is also involved in the process and executes the customer's order)" [R2, RG 36.44]. That the user holds their own brokerage account does not take the app out of this.
- Indicators ASIC weighs: the transaction "would probably not take place" without you, you "add value", and you receive benefits depending on the client's decisions; "a question of degree" [R2, RG 36.42-36.46]. A subscription or per-trade fee tied to trading strengthens the case.
- **Dealing as agent** (placing an order in the user's name with power to bind them) is dealing proper, needing an authorisation to "deal ... on behalf of another", which "also covers arranging activities" [R2, RG 36.49]. An app submitting orders with the user's OAuth token or API key may sit here rather than in mere arranging: lawyer question.
- **Licence or representative:** "You will need to hold an AFS licence if you carry on a financial services business in this jurisdiction, unless an exemption in s911A(2) ... applies. For example, due to the exemption in s911A(2)(a), you do not need an AFS licence to provide financial services as a representative of a licensee" [R2, RG 36.13]. Practical routes: own AFSL, or become an authorised representative of an existing licensee whose licence covers the activity. ASIC's digital-asset relief also refers to "intermediary authorisation arrangements with an AFS licence holder" [R8].
- US-listed shares and ETFs are securities, hence financial products, wherever they are listed (general position; confirm with lawyer). Whether the executing broker (for example Alpaca Securities LLC) is itself licensed or exempt to serve Australian retail clients was **not researched: UNCONFIRMED**.
- Not arranging: "merely providing purely factual information" and business-introduction services where the operator "does not play a significant role and does not benefit from investment decisions made by consumers" [R2, RG 36.44, 36.47]. A deep link that opens the broker's own app, with the order typed there, is closer to this end than in-app order entry.

## 2. AI-generated research, signals and trade proposals

- **Advice test:** "a recommendation or a statement of opinion ... that is intended to, or can reasonably be regarded as being intended to, influence a client in making a decision about a particular financial product or class of financial product ... is financial product advice." Purely factual information ("objectively ascertainable information whose truth or accuracy cannot be reasonably questioned") generally is not [R2, RG 36.23]. Buy/sell signals and trade proposals are recommendations.
- **Personal vs general:** personal advice is advice where the provider "has considered one or more of the person's objectives, financial situation and needs" or "a reasonable person might expect the provider to have considered one or more of those matters (s766B(3)). All other financial product advice is general advice" [R2, RG 36.97]. An agent that reads the user's holdings, cash, limits or goals before proposing a trade is likely on the personal side.
- **Digital advice is licensed the same way.** ASIC's example of an app recommending model portfolios with stock commentary and no personal questions is "general advice and not factual information"; in both its general and personal examples "the digital advice provider must hold an AFS licence" [R3, RG 255.27-255.28]. RG 255 also requires "at least one responsible manager who meets the minimum training and competence standards that apply to advisers" [R3, RG 255.36], and says algorithm testing "should be carried out before any digital advice is provided to clients, and on an ongoing basis after that" [R3, note under the licensee questions; see also RG 255.72-255.74].
- **General advice warning:** "if general advice is provided to a retail client, the providing entity must warn the client that the advice does not take account of the client's objectives, financial situation or needs: see s949A" [R2, RG 36.100]. The warning does not remove the licensing requirement.
- **Personal advice to a retail client** adds a Statement of Advice (s946A), best-interests duty (s961B), appropriate advice (s961G), warnings (s961H) and conflict priority (s961J) [R2, RG 36.98-36.99].
- Remuneration matters: advice is "more likely" found where the provider "is remunerated by the client or stands to benefit depending on the decisions made by a client" [R2, RG 36.22].
- AI content gets no special treatment: the law applies "in the same way to AI-generated advertising content as human-generated content", and hallucinated or biased output "may increase the risk of misleading or deceptive advertising" [R15, RG 234.5 note]. ASIC guidance specific to AI trading agents was not found: UNCONFIRMED whether any exists.

## 3. An AI agent that trades automatically within user-set limits

- **Definition.** An MDA is "a facility, other than a registered managed investment scheme ..., with the following features: (a) a person (MDA client) makes contributions; (b) the client portfolio assets are managed on an individual basis by another person (MDA provider) at the MDA provider's discretion, subject to any agreed limitation; and (c) the client and the MDA provider intend that the MDA provider will use the client portfolio assets to generate a financial return" [R4, RG 179.13].
- **User-set limits do not avoid it** ("subject to any agreed limitation"). The trigger is "discretion to make investments without prior reference to the client for each transaction" [R4, RG 179.18]. Retaining legal title does not avoid it either: contributions include "giving the MDA provider access to, and control of, these funds and assets ... through a power of attorney or other authorisation" [R4, RG 179.16], and the client is treated as having "no day-to-day control ... even if the client has the legal title to those assets" [R4, RG 179.20]. An API key or OAuth trading scope is plausibly such an authorisation.
- **What ASIC says an MDA is:** generally both a "managed investment scheme" and a "facility for making a financial investment", with conditional relief under **ASIC Corporations (Managed Discretionary Account Services) Instrument 2026/720** [R4, RG 179.6-179.7]. The register shows 2026/720 made 24 September 2026 and in force; Instrument 2016/968 is repealed [R5]. RG 179 was reissued 30 September 2026 [R4].
- **Requirements to rely on the relief** [R4, RG 179.14 and Tables 1-2]: an AFS licence authorising (a) issuing MDA interests, (b) dealing in everything acquired for the portfolio, (c) personal advice about the MDA (unless an external MDA adviser contracts with each client), (d) custody (unless an external custodian does); an MDA contract entered "before providing the MDA", containing an investment program; a statement that you will act honestly and in the client's best interests, a risk warning where non-limited-recourse products are used, fee disclosure, termination steps; review of the contract and program "at least once every 13 months"; FSG content.
- **Wholesale-only:** "If you offer or issue an MDA only to wholesale clients, you do not need to rely on our relief" because the scheme and disclosure provisions apply only to retail clients [R4, RG 179.36]; licensing for dealing and advice still applies (lawyer to confirm scope). Accounts "operated for providing non-discretionary trading services (e.g. those operated by brokers) are not MDA services because they are not used for services involving discretionary trading" [R4, RG 179.16 note 1].
- **Per-order approval** keeps "prior reference to the client for each transaction" and so stays outside the trading-discretion limb; it remains arranging/dealing (section 1) and advice (section 2).
- Crypto: RG 179 speaks of investing "in financial products". Whether discretionary trading of crypto that is not a financial product is still a managed investment scheme was not resolved: UNCONFIRMED, lawyer question.

## 4. Crypto and digital assets (status at November 2026)

**Current law: token-by-token (ASIC INFO 225)**
- INFO 225 "Digital assets: Financial products and services" "was updated in October 2025. Minor edits ... were made in April 2026" [R6]. It tests each asset against managed investment scheme, security, derivative, non-cash payment facility and financial-investment facility, with 18 worked examples.
- Bitcoin (Example 5) is unlikely to be a non-cash payment facility and "also unlikely to be any other type of financial product" [R6]. Its examples treat yield-bearing stablecoins, many wrapped tokens ("likely to be derivatives"), some managed staking and tokenised bonds as likely financial products; distributors of eligible stablecoins and wrapped tokens have class relief (ASIC Corporations (Stablecoin and Wrapped Token Relief) Instrument 2025/867) [R6].
- Intermediaries: "Arranging for another person to deal in financial products is also a form of dealing. For example, a business that assists people with buying or selling digital assets that are financial products may itself be dealing in financial products (e.g. acting as a broker)" [R6]. Advice on such assets is financial product advice.
- **No-action position has closed to new entrants.** ASIC's class no-action letter covered unlicensed digital-asset services only where an AFS licence application was lodged by 30 June 2026 [R7], later extended to 30 September 2026 and widened to authorised-representative and intermediary-authorisation arrangements [R8]. ASIC: "Entities will need to comply with existing licensing requirements throughout the implementation of the law reforms" [R9]. Whether any further extension exists: UNCONFIRMED (none found).

**Digital Assets Framework legislation: passed, not yet commenced**
- Corporations Amendment (Digital Assets Framework) Act 2026, No. 38 of 2026, assented to 8 April 2026 [R10]; ASIC says it "passed Parliament on 1 April 2026" [R9].
- **Commencement date conflict.** The Act commences "the day after the end of the period of 12 months beginning on the day this Act receives the Royal Assent"; the register's commencement table shows **8 April 2027** (column 3 "is not part of this Act") [R10]. ASIC's roadmap says it "will commence on **9 April 2027**" [R9]. Treat as 8-9 April 2027 until a lawyer confirms.
- It makes a "digital asset platform" (operator possesses digital tokens for or on behalf of a client) and a "tokenised custody platform" financial products (new s761GC, s761GD, s764A(1)(la),(lb)) [R10]. Exchanges holding customer crypto therefore become AFS-licensed product issuers; dealing in, and advising on, the *platform* become financial services.
- Exemptions added to s911A(2) [R10]: (ja) small platforms (no more than $10 million transactions in 12 months, no more than $5,000 per client, notice lodged with ASIC); **(jb) a service consisting only of "advising another person about the existence of a digital asset platform" or "arranging for another person to use a digital asset platform or tokenised custody platform"**, in the ordinary course of business where "such services are not a significant part of the person's business". An app whose main feature is crypto trading may not meet "not a significant part": lawyer question.
- Transition: 6 months from commencement; a person who applies for a licence in that period is not caught until ASIC decides (new s1731-1732) [R10]. ASIC plans guidance in months 6-12 of its roadmap and applications in months 12-18 [R9].

**AUSTRAC (AML/CTF)**
- "Virtual asset" and "virtual asset service provider" (VASP) replaced "digital currency" and "digital currency exchange provider" [R11]. Designated services: exchanging virtual assets for money "or make arrangements for this type of exchange" (item 50A); virtual-to-virtual exchange or arrangements (50B); safekeeping (46A); transfers on behalf of customers (items 29-30); financial services connected with an offer or sale (50C) [R11].
- Dates: item 50A obligations apply from 31 March 2026; other new virtual asset services are deferred to 1 July 2026; a provider of only new services that applied for registration before 29 July 2026 may continue until AUSTRAC decides [R13][R14].
- "You must be registered with us before you can provide virtual asset services in Australia. It's illegal to provide virtual asset services in Australia without being registered" [R12].
- **Does merely routing orders to a registered exchange need registration? Not settled by the guidance.** For: items 50A/50B now cover "making arrangements", so "a VASP won't need to provide every element of the exchange", and captured activity "may also include ... another intermediary facilitating the transaction where such services are provided by a virtual asset service provider" [R11]. Against: 46A/50A/50B apply only "in the course of carrying on a business as a virtual asset service provider" [R11], and AUSTRAC's software carve-out ("a person who solely provides a software application ... but doesn't engage in safekeeping or administration") is stated for safekeeping, not exchange [R11]. Needs written legal advice or an AUSTRAC query before any crypto order routing.
- An AFS licensee that "arranges" for customers to receive designated services has its own AML/CTF category (item 54), with reduced obligations referenced by AUSTRAC [R14]. Details: UNCONFIRMED.

## 5. Paper trading, simulated portfolios and backtests

- Misleading or deceptive conduct in relation to financial services is prohibited under the ASIC Act (s12DA, s12DB) and Corporations Act (s1041H, s1041E); forward-looking statements without reasonable grounds are taken to be misleading (s769C) [R15, RG 234.109 and Appendix]. The Australian Consumer Law's equivalent provisions do not apply to financial services or financial products (Competition and Consumer Act 2010, s131A) [R18]; they do apply to the non-financial parts of the app.
- ASIC's advertising guide was **reissued 9 June 2026** and RG 53 (past performance) was withdrawn the same day [R15][R16]. It applies to "internet-based applications" and social media [R15, RG 234.5].
- **Past performance:** promotions "should draw attention (unambiguously and without reservation) to the fact that the past performance is not indicative of future performance", on the same page, legible, not buried. Suggested wording: "Past performance is not a reliable indicator of future performance". Wording such as "not a guarantee of future performance" is "less likely to be effective" [R15, RG 234.83-234.88].
- **Non-actual performance** ("hypothetical", "simulated", "modelled"; "indicative" is discouraged): "Promoters should exercise extreme care"; it is more likely misleading where assumptions are significant, unavailable to the audience, or chosen "using the benefit of hindsight"; "'back-testing' data may give a misleading impression" [R15, RG 234.111-234.116]. ASIC says the only generally safe uses are a fund identical to an existing one, or an index mirror [R15, RG 234.117]. Non-actual returns must be "clearly separated from the actual returns and adequately explained"; footnotes are "unlikely to be effective" [R15, RG 234.119].
- Practical consequences for the app: label paper and backtest results as simulated on the same screen; state the fill, fee, slippage and dividend assumptions (Alpaca's paper engine ignores fees, slippage, queue position and dividends: see provider-capabilities.md); never mix simulated and real returns in one figure; do not use paper or backtest returns of an AI agent to promote the live agent without legal review; no forecasts without reasonable grounds.

## 6. Design and distribution, privacy, KYC/AML

- **DDO (Corporations Act Pt 7.8A):** applies to issuers and distributors; distributors are "regulated persons, including AFS licensees [and] authorised representatives". A distributor must not engage in "retail product distribution conduct" unless a target market determination (TMD) exists or is not required, and must take "reasonable steps" so distribution is consistent with the TMD (s994E(3)) [R17]. "Excluded financial products include ... most ordinary shares" [R17, Figure 1]. An MDA service is itself a financial product issued to retail clients, so expect to make a TMD for it (lawyer to confirm).
- **Privacy Act 1988:** a "small business" (annual turnover $3,000,000 or less) is generally exempt, but not if it "discloses personal information about another individual to anyone else for a benefit, service or advantage" or "provides a benefit, service or advantage to collect personal information" (s6D(4)(c)-(d)) [R19]. Plan to comply regardless: brokerage holdings, balances and order history are personal information; the APPs (Schedule 1) cover a privacy policy, collection notices, use and disclosure limits, overseas disclosure (US brokers, SnapTrade, AI model providers) and security; the notifiable data breaches scheme is Part IIIC [R19]. The register shows the current compilation ending 10 December 2026, so amendments start that day: content UNCONFIRMED. OAIC guidance pages refused automated access: not reviewed.
- **KYC/AML:** the reporting entity that provides the designated service performs customer due diligence: "You must also complete necessary steps to know your customer before you start to provide them with a designated service" [R11]. Where the user's account is at the broker or exchange, that entity onboards and verifies the user (for Alpaca's Broker API "trading app" model Alpaca runs KYC; provider-capabilities.md [A22]). The app would carry its own obligations only if its activity is a designated service (section 4) or it becomes a licensee arranging designated services (item 54).
- Storing users' exchange API keys or broker tokens is a security obligation under APP 11 and the providers' terms; request trade-only scopes with no withdrawal permission.

## 7. Apple App Store Review Guidelines (page "Last Updated: October 16, 2026") [R20]

- **3.2.1(viii)**: "Apps used for financial trading, investing, or money management should be submitted by the financial institution performing such services and must have necessary licensing and permissions in the locations where you make them available."
- **5.1.1(ix)**: "Apps that provide services in highly regulated fields (such as banking and financial services, healthcare, gambling, legal cannabis use, air travel and crypto exchanges) or that require sensitive user information should be submitted by a legal entity that provides the services, and not by an individual developer."
- **3.1.5(iii)** Exchanges: "Apps may facilitate transactions or transmissions of cryptocurrency on an approved exchange, provided they are offered only in countries or regions where the app has appropriate licensing and permissions to provide a cryptocurrency exchange." **3.1.5(i)** Wallets: only "developers enrolled as an organization". **3.1.5(iv)**: ICOs, "cryptocurrency futures trading, and other crypto-securities or quasi-securities trading must come from established banks, securities firms, futures commission merchants ('FCM'), or other approved financial institutions". **3.1.5(v)**: no currency "for completing tasks".
- **3.2.2(viii)**: binary options apps not permitted; CFD and other derivatives (such as FOREX) apps "must be properly licensed in all jurisdictions where the service is available".
- **5 (Legal)**: "Apps must comply with all legal requirements in any location where you make them available (if you're not sure, check with a lawyer)."
- **5.1.2(i)**: "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so."
- **2.5.19**: "Don't include any hidden, dormant, or undocumented features in your app". A live-trading switch that ships dark must be described in the review notes (2.3.1(a) requires new features to be "described with specificity").

## 8. Go-live gate

Every condition must be verifiably true (document, register entry, test log or screenshot on file) before the server-side flag for that level is turned on. Each level includes all conditions of the level before it.

**(a) Manual live orders**
1. Written legal advice characterises in-app order entry (arranging vs dealing as agent vs neither) for each asset class, and names the licensing route: own AFSL, authorised representative of a named licensee, or a specific exemption.
2. The licence or authorisation appears on ASIC's public register and its number and required disclosures are shown in the app.
3. For each execution venue, evidence that it may serve Australian retail clients, and its terms permit third-party order entry.
4. Provider approval in writing: Alpaca Connect app approved for live trading, or SnapTrade production key with trading approved, or the exchange's partner/OAuth approval.
5. Countries and asset classes enabled match the adapter capability table, with no UNCONFIRMED entries; AU-resident eligibility confirmed by each provider.
6. Credentials are trade-only: no withdrawal or transfer permission, verified by API at connect time and rejected otherwise; stored server-side, never in the app bundle or logs.
7. Order types, TIFs, precision and minimums validated per adapter on paper/sandbox/dry-run, including partial fills, rejects, cancels and reconnects.
8. Every order passes a confirmation screen showing account, symbol, side, quantity or notional, type, limit/stop, TIF, estimated cost and fees with their source, then an explicit confirm action.
9. Idempotent client order IDs; order state reconciled from stream plus polling; append-only audit log of every instruction and provider response.
10. Crypto: a token allow-list reviewed against INFO 225, plus written advice on AUSTRAC registration for order routing (section 4).
11. DDO position documented (distributor or not; TMD checks if so).
12. Privacy policy and collection notices cover brokerage data, overseas recipients and AI processing; breach response plan exists; in-app consent for third-party AI sharing.
13. App Store: organisation account, the submitting entity is the one holding the licence or authorisation, storefronts limited to licensed regions, review notes describe live trading.
14. Global and per-user kill switches tested; complaints and support contact live.

**(b) Approval-required agent orders** (agent proposes, user approves each order)
15. Written legal advice classifies agent proposals as general or personal advice; licence authorisations match.
16. If general: the s949A warning is shown with every proposal and nothing in the UI implies the user's circumstances were considered. If personal: Statement of Advice, best-interests process, and a responsible manager meeting adviser competence standards are in place.
17. Algorithm governance per RG 255: documented testing before release and ongoing, change control, human review process, logged inputs/outputs for each proposal.
18. One approval authorises exactly one order with fixed parameters and an expiry; no standing or batch approvals.
19. Proposals show data sources and time of data; no performance claims from paper or backtests without section 5 labelling.
20. Provider rule met: order-confirmation flow with all available impact, fee and commission information (SnapTrade launch checklist).

**(c) Autonomous agent orders**
21. Written legal advice on MDA status under RG 179 and Instrument 2026/720; if it is an MDA: licence authorisations in RG 179.14 held (or external MDA adviser/custodian contracts), MDA contract with investment program signed before activation, personal advice on suitability given, 13-month review cycle scheduled, FSG updated, TMD made.
22. Retail vs wholesale client handling decided and enforced.
23. Crypto autonomy separately cleared (managed investment scheme question; Digital Assets Framework Act position after April 2027; AUSTRAC).
24. User-set limits enforced server-side: allowed instruments, max order and position size, daily loss and order-count limits, trading hours; limits cannot be raised by the agent.
25. Instant pause and revoke by the user; revocation deletes the provider connection or key; every fill notified to the user.
26. Provider consent for automated trading recorded (SnapTrade: "clear user consent for automated trading"); provider rate limits respected.
27. Insurance, dispute-resolution membership and financial requirements attached to the licence are met (lawyer to list).

## 9. Not legal advice; items needing a lawyer's sign-off

This document is engineering research assembled from public regulator and legislation pages on 2026-11-13. It is not legal advice, it may misread the sources, and the law is changing (RG 179, RG 234 and the MDA instrument were all reissued in 2026; the Digital Assets Framework Act commences in April 2027). An Australian financial services lawyer must sign off, at minimum: gate items 1, 3, 10, 11, 15, 16, 21, 22, 23 and 27; the dealing-as-agent question; whether the executing brokers may serve AU retail clients; the s911A(2)(jb) exemption; the AUSTRAC "making arrangements" question; the commencement-date conflict; Privacy Act coverage and the December 2026 amendments; and the wording of all warnings.
